## What Is Zero-Party Data From a Quiz?

Zero-party data is information a shopper hands you directly, on purpose, in response to a question you asked. A quiz answer about skin type, gift budget, or fit preference is zero-party data. A page view or an abandoned cart is inferred behavior, a guess about intent built from what the shopper did, not what they said.

The distinction matters for personalization quality. Inferred data can be wrong, since a shopper might browse a product for a friend, not themselves. Quiz answers carry stated intent, which makes them a stronger input for the emails and texts that follow. ## Why Flows Outperform One-Off Campaigns

A flow is a triggered, automated sequence, like a welcome series or a post-quiz follow-up. A campaign is a one-off send to a broader list. The performance gap between the two is large and consistent in 2026 benchmark data.

| Metric | One-off campaigns | Automated flows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average click rate | 1.69% | 5.58% |
| Average placed-order rate | 0.16% | 2.11% |
| Share of email revenue | 59% of sends | 41% of revenue from 5.3% of sends |

Flows outperform because they trigger on a specific, recent action, like finishing a quiz, instead of reaching an entire list at an arbitrary time ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/products/email-marketing/benchmarks)). A quiz result is one of the clearest trigger events available, since the shopper just told you exactly what they are looking for.

## Turning Quiz Answers Into a Segmented Flow

Tag each quiz answer to the shopper's profile as soon as they complete the quiz, not as a batch job run later. The tag becomes the segmentation key for every message that follows, so a shopper who said "sensitive skin" never receives a promotion for a fragrance-heavy product line.

Build the first message to trigger immediately after quiz completion, referencing the specific result by name. Space follow-up messages over the next one to four weeks rather than sending everything on day one, since about 1 in 5 quiz-attributed orders land more than 30 days after the quiz was taken ([RevenueHunt](https://revenuehunt.com/state-of-product-recommendation-quizzes/)). A single post-quiz email captures the shopper who buys immediately and misses everyone who needed more time. The [quiz design guide](/blog/shopify-product-recommendation-quiz/) covers the quiz side of this in detail, including how to capture answers correctly at the point of completion.

## What to Send After Each Quiz Result

| Trigger | Message |
| --- | --- |
| Quiz completed | Immediate result email with the specific recommendation and reasoning |
| No purchase after 3 days | Reminder email or SMS referencing the exact quiz result, not a generic nudge |
| No purchase after 10 days | Social proof or review content for the recommended product |
| No purchase after 30 days | Reintroduce the quiz with a fresh incentive, since preferences may have shifted |
| Purchase completed | Cross-sell tied to the original quiz answers, not a random bestseller |

Each message should reference the quiz answer directly. A generic "come back and shop" email throws away the zero-party data the shopper already gave you.

## Metrics That Matter More Than Open Rate

Open rate has become a weaker signal since Apple Mail Privacy Protection began pre-loading tracking pixels, a change that now affects roughly 64% of B2C email subscribers and inflates reported open rates regardless of whether a shopper actually read the email ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/uk/blog/email-marketing-benchmarks-open-click-and-conversion-rates)). Click rate and placed-order rate are not subject to the same distortion, since both require a real action past the inbox.

For quiz-triggered flows specifically, track placed-order rate segmented by quiz answer, not just flow-wide. A flow that performs well on average can still be underperforming for one specific quiz segment, and that gap only shows up when you break the data down instead of reading a single blended number.
